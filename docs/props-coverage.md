# Battle-map prop coverage

A list of the kinds of objects top-down battle maps need, grouped by family and by
setting. It was put together from how published asset libraries and map tools organise
their catalogues. Those catalogues were used only as lists of ideas: no art was
downloaded, looked at closely, copied or traced. Every name here is a plain, generic
object kind.

## Sources surveyed

- **Forgotten Adventures** (https://www.forgotten-adventures.net/product-category/assets/,
  https://new.forgotten-adventures.net/?p=3738): a library of more than 140,000 assets.
  Since 2025 it has been organised in folders by biome (Arctic, Desert, Woodlands and so
  on), with themed expansion packs (Aquatic, Mountain, Desert). Its Dungeondraft packs
  sort objects into Set dressing, Furniture, Clutter, Structures, Vehicles and Encounter
  pieces, plus textures (Floors, Terrain, Paths, Walls).
- **Dungeondraft** (built-in library plus community packs): objects are browsed in a
  Library panel with tags. Built-in tag groups cover furniture, nature, lights,
  structures, clutter and dungeon pieces. Community packs add their own tag sets.
- **2-Minute Tabletop** (https://2minutetabletop.com/dungeondraft/): themed packs, each
  a setting or a family. Examples are Furniture (chairs, tables, beds and oddments),
  Castle Furniture, Food, Statues, Broken Furniture, Battle Damage, Blood & Bodies,
  Dungeon Traps, Bandit Camp, Farmer's Field, Desert, Painted Tundra, Metalsmith's
  Workshop, Mushroom Village, Fallen Tree, Forest Fungi, Natural Walls and Cliffs,
  Ocean Water and Treasure.
- **Dungeon Alchemist** (https://www.dungeonalchemist.com/): an AI room-filler with more
  than 6,000 objects. It is organised by **theme** (tavern, mansion, castle, crypt,
  forest, island and others) and **room type** (bedroom, kitchen and so on). Each room
  type has a furnishing rule set, which makes it a good model for setting-aware dressing.
- **Inkarnate** (https://feedback.inkarnate.com/changelog/inkarnate-20-star): battle-map
  stamps grouped by style pack and setting: Castle, Dungeon, Camp, Tavern and Dwarven.
- **Tom Cartos** (Dungeondraft Megapack): about 18,000 assets tagged into 6 groups:
  Artificial Assets, Buildings, Floors, Modular Assets, Natural Assets and Prefabs.
- **Czepeku** (https://www.czepeku.com/blog/navigating-our-fantasy-maps-gallery): finished
  maps rather than loose assets, browsed by **theme** (pastoral, arcane machinery and
  similar), tag and map type. Useful as a checklist of settings people actually play in.
- **Others seen in passing:** token and prop packs on itch.io and Foundry (furniture
  token packs, modern props) and the Roll20 marketplace "building essentials" sets. All
  of them repeat the same families: furniture, clutter, nature, structures, lights and
  vehicles.

**Common pattern:** every catalogue has two axes. One is **family** (furniture, clutter,
nature, structures, lights, vehicles). The other is **setting or biome** (tavern,
castle, dungeon, camp, desert, arctic). Variants within a family come from material,
size, wear and contents. This file follows the same two axes.

## Priorities

Ranked by coverage per unit of work: how many settings a family serves, and how many
variants one parametric drawing gives.

1. **Containers** (barrels, crates, sacks, chests, baskets, jars): in nearly every setting;
   one family with material, size, lid, contents and damage gives dozens of variants.
2. **Clutter and floor dressing**: what makes any room look lived-in (Matt's main bar);
   small, cheap shapes that dressing can scatter in thousands.
3. **Rubble, debris and damage**: ruins, dungeons, caves, mines, sewers and battles all
   need it; procedural chunks are natural for code.
4. **Tables and work surfaces**: a parametric frame (shape, size, legs, material) covers
   desks, counters, benches and altars.
5. **Seating**: chairs, stools and benches go with every table; a small parametric set.
6. **Light sources**: needed by every interior and by step 10 lighting as data.
7. **Shelving and storage furniture**: one frame plus "contents" (books, jars, bottles,
   tools) serves libraries, shops, kitchens, labs and pantries.
8. **Outdoor vegetation** (bushes, grass, ferns, flowers, reeds): every outdoor map;
   parametric by species, size and season.
9. **Trees, rocks and terrain features**: outdoor maps and caves; boulders and stalagmites
   share a lot of drawing.
10. **Textiles** (rugs, banners, curtains, tapestries): large flat shapes, cheap to draw,
    huge visual payoff, parametric by colour and pattern.
11. **Tableware and small surface goods**: the density inside rooms; small and numerous,
    fed by surface dressing.
12. **Kitchen and food**: taverns, homes, kitchens, markets and camps; reuses containers
    and tableware.
13. **Books and paper**: libraries, studies, temples, shops, guardhouses; tiny, many
    variants.
14. **Beds and sleeping**: homes, inns, barracks, prisons, camps, manors; one frame family.
15. **Hearths and fire**: the centre of most interiors and camps; ties into lighting.
16. **Tools by trade**: forges, farms, mines, workshops, docks; one "tool on a rack or
    bench" approach covers many trades.
17. **Weapons, armour and racks**: barracks, guardhouses, throne rooms, camps, battlefields.
18. **Structures and fixtures** (stairs, ladders, trapdoors, columns, grates, fences): needed
    in almost every map to show ways in and out.
19. **Water features**: wells, fountains, pools and channels for towns, sewers, swamps and
    baths; may build on existing ground regions.
20. **Bones and remains**: dungeons, caves, crypts, lairs and deserts; a small kit of bone
    shapes that combine.
21. **Statues and monuments**: temples, ruins, halls, graveyards; a plinth plus a shape,
    with damage variants.
22. **Graveyard and crypt** (gravestones, coffins, sarcophagi): a well-defined setting with
    simple shapes.
23. **Mining** (rails, carts, supports, ore): opens mines and caverns; pairs with cave
    walls in step 11.
24. **Magic and alchemy** (glassware, circles, curios): labs, towers and cult lairs; much
    of it reuses jars and bottles.
25. **Market stalls and camp gear**: awning and tent shapes plus existing goods; unlocks
    markets, camps and docks cheaply.

After these: nautical, farm, animal things, machinery, vehicles, prisons, bath,
entertainment and treasure. Each serves fewer settings, but most reuse families above.
